## Moykr: Prescriptive versus Propositional Knowledge

::: fl

**Prescriptive Knowledge** ($\lambda$-knowledge): 

- Knowledge of [**how** to do something]{.h}. 


It is the **recipe**, **technique**, or **invention** itself. 	

- Example: Yoghurt
	- Adding a spoonful of starter yoghurt to warm milk will transform it overnight. No understanding of microbiology is required; one simply has to follow the steps.

&nbsp; 

- Example: Social Networks
	- How do social networks work over long distance?
	- How do you shape beliefs?

:::

**Propositional Knowledge** ($\omega$-knowledge): 

- Knowledge of [**why** something works.]{.h} 
	
	- It consists of scientific principles, natural laws, and systematic explanations. 
	
		- In this case of yoghurt, it is the biological and chemical understanding of bacterial fermentation, enzyme activity, and protein coagulation.

## What AI can help with

::: fl

#### 1. Concept Explanation & Literature Mapping 

- Tools can explain complex methods (e.g., ANOVA, mixed-effects models, p-values), suggest study resources, or help identify analytical frameworks used in published papers. 

#### 2. Specialised Scientific Discovery 

- Platforms like Anthropic Claude Science help integrate fragmented databases, literature, and analytical tools in fields like biomedicine and formal sciences.

:::

::: fr


#### 3. Data Cleaning & Preprocessing 

- AI can draft cleaning scripts, parse unstructured or mixed data, and identify structural issues—provided a human reviews the actual transformations.

#### 4. Code Generation and Debugging 

- Instead of performing math directly, most modern AI tools write and execute code (such as Python, R, or Stata) to run data transformations and statistical tests. 

:::

## Major Risks and Limitations


::: fl

#### Hallucinations and Methodological Misalignment

- LLMs predict text and code sequences based on patterns; they are not trained statisticians. They may apply the wrong statistical test to your data structure or misinterpret edge cases while sounding completely confident. 

#### Causal and Contextual Blind Spots 

- AI struggles with deep causal logic, study design nuances, and qualitative context, often favoring overly complex or popular methods over appropriate ones (complexity and popularity bias). 

:::

::: fr

#### Data Privacy and Security 

- Uploading proprietary, sensitive, or personal research datasets into public conversational AI instances risks data memorisation or leakage. 

&nbsp; 

#### The Code Verification Bottleneck 

- As noted in analyses on Medium, using AI for data analysis means you are really reviewing the code it wrote; if you cannot read or audit the code yourself, you cannot safely trust the output. 

:::

##  Best Practices for Using AI Responsibly

::: fl

#### Keep a Human-in-the-Loop 

- Treat AI as a research assistant, not the principal investigator. 
	- You are fully [responsible]{.h} for the methodology, ethics, and validity of the findings.

#### Inspect and Run Code Independently 

- Do not rely on black-box answers.  
	- Have the AI generate clean scripts (Python/R), and execute and verify them outside the chat interface.

:::

::: fr

#### Use Prescriptive Prompting

- Avoid vague exploratory prompts that let the AI guess how to analyze your data. 
	- Explicitly state the variables, hypotheses, and exact statistical models you want tested.

#### Protect Data Privacy 

- Use local, [open-source models]{.h} on private infrastructure or institutional enterprise tools compliant with data protection policies when working with sensitive datasets. 

:::
